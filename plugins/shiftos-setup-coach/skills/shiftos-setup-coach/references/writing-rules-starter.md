# ShiftOS Writing Rules — Starter Set

The Setup Coach writes `ME/writing-rules.md` in Step 4 using this starter set as the universal half, then adds the personal half from the user's answers. The starter rules ship as-is — the user doesn't configure them, though they can strike any rule they disagree with.

---

## The starter set (write into the file intact)

### Banned words and phrases

These read as machine-written or as marketing filler. They don't appear in anything that ships — drafts, outlines, or talking points.

**The AI shelf:** leverage · streamline · empower · elevate · robust · seamless · delve · landscape (as in "the marketing landscape") · journey (unless someone is literally traveling) · unlock · supercharge · game-changer · cutting-edge · in today's fast-paced world · it's important to note · at the end of the day

**The destination-cliché shelf:** hidden gem · something for everyone · nestled · boasts (a destination never "boasts" anything) · breathtaking (pick a detail instead) · world-class (prove it or drop it) · vibrant (as a default adjective) · must-see / must-visit · bucket list

**The padding shelf:** very · really · just (as a softener) · arguably · various · numerous · in order to (use "to") · the fact that

### Patterns to kill

- **The rule-of-three reflex.** Not every sentence needs three parallel items. If two carry it, use two.
- **The mirrored opener.** "It's not just X — it's Y." Once a month at most.
- **Throat-clearing.** Delete any opening sentence that could begin any document on any topic. Start where the substance starts.
- **The summary sandwich.** Don't restate what was just said as a closing paragraph. End on the last new thing.
- **Hedging stacks.** One qualifier per claim, maximum. "It could potentially perhaps suggest" is three ways of saying nothing.
- **Fake transitions.** "Moreover," "Furthermore," and "Additionally" at paragraph heads. If two paragraphs belong together, the content shows it.
- **Exclamation inflation.** Professional writing in this workspace earns one exclamation point a quarter.

### Formatting law

- Bottom line first. The reader knows the recommendation before they know the reasoning.
- Numbers carry units, timeframes, and sources. "Up 12% YoY (STR, March)" — never a naked percentage.
- Bullets for parallel facts; prose for reasoning; tables for comparisons. Don't bullet an argument.
- One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a second comma, audition it as two.
- Headings say what the section concludes, not what it covers. "Co-op renewals are at risk" beats "Co-op update."
- Anything board-facing or partner-facing ships final-polish: no placeholders, no [TBD], no first-draft tone.

### The pre-ship check

Before any piece of writing leaves a session, verify:

1. Zero banned words and phrases.
2. The reader gets the point in the first two sentences.
3. Every number has a source and a timeframe.
4. It sounds like the owner of this workspace — check the voice profile if unsure.
5. Cut 10% — there's always 10%.

## The personal half (collected during setup)

The coach fills these sections from the user's answers. Specificity is the whole game — vague entries here produce the generic voice these rules exist to prevent.

- **My register** — two to four words in the user's own phrasing. Weak: "professional but friendly." Strong: "direct, warm, allergic to hype — reads like a memo from someone you trust."
- **My phrases** — things they actually say, captured verbatim from the voice dump and conversation. Weak: "I'm pretty casual." Strong: "the math has to math" · "let's not bury the lede" · opens emails with "Quick one —".
- **My additions to the banned list** — their destination's worn-out words, the board's pet peeves, terms the agency overuses. Weak: "no clichés." Strong: "never 'gateway to the Rockies' (every neighbor claims it) · the board chair hates 'synergy' · we say 'partners,' never 'members.'"
- **Conventions** — spelling (US/UK), serial comma, date format, how their organization's name is written on first and later references.
- **Audience flags** — one line per audience where the rules tighten or loosen: e.g., "board: no contractions, no humor" · "partners: contractions fine, first names."

## Coaching notes

- If they offer a vague register, ask how a trusted colleague would describe their emails — that answer is usually the real register.
- The fastest source of natural phrases is the Step 3 voice dump. Quote it back to them: "you said 'the math has to math' — does that belong in your file?"
- If they push back on a starter rule, strike it for them without debate and note the override in the file. Their rules, their call.
